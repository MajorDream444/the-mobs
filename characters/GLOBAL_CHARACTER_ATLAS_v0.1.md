## Inside each Mob: twelve-seat development grid
Keep the existing twelve positions per Mob and reconcile leader placement before assigning IDs. This document does not add a thirteenth seat to any team. Populate positions from recovered characters first; create new identities only for actual gaps.
Every Mob should include:
- Multiple geographic and linguistic perspectives, including diaspora and cross-cultural experience.
- A range of adult life-stage presentations: early-career, mid-career and elder mentorship.
- Different communication styles: reflective and expressive, direct and diplomatic, exploratory and methodical.
- Different education stories, including formal study, apprenticeship, community learning and self-directed practice.
- Complementary decision habits: evidence checking, opportunity sensing, prototyping, facilitation and consequences review.
- Accessibility and differing levels of technical comfort in examples and user testing.

These are composition goals, not a rule that a nationality or age predicts skill or temperament. Do not assign an entire culture a personality. English can be a bridge language, but no client should have to perform fluency to contribute.

## Individual dossier schema
Stable character ID; display name; Mob and role; source provenance; design status; proposed cultural connections and story base; apparent age/life stage; intended languages with tested proficiency stored separately; temperament dimensions; communication preferences; values; motivations; fears and limitations; expertise areas; practical demonstrations; influence blend; signature methods; learning relationships; client participation; development history.

Credentials must distinguish fictional story-world education, actual model/source training material, and independently demonstrated runtime capability. Do not publish invented real-university degrees, licenses, employment histories or endorsements. A credible profile can show a sample assignment, method, result and limit.

## Evolution model
Keep a stable identity with versioned changes to skills, beliefs, methods and roles. Record the experience, evidence and reason for each change. Maintain meaningful differences between Mobs as they share lessons. A changed specialty is permitted; silently rewriting origin or past decisions is not continuity.
Each quarterly character review asks:
1. What did this character learn?
2. Which belief or method changed, and why?
3. Who did they learn from or teach?
4. What remains uncertain?
5. Does their public profile still match demonstrated capability?
Founder approval governs major identity and role changes. Knowledge-sharing implementation remains future engineering.
