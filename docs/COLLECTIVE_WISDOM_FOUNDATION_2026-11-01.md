Date: 1 November 2026
Status: founder direction recorded; influence assignments and implementation design proposed.

## Founder direction
Major Dream Williams wants relatable, inspiring AI leaders with extraordinary abilities. They support people in changing their mindset, seeing possibilities, and executing their own vision. “We don't do your vision for you. We execute your vision.”

Major now has a dedicated development team. His intended division is the first 30%: opportunity discovery, relationships, vision, initial concepts and prototypes. The team takes the remaining 70% through engineering and delivery. These percentages describe a working division of responsibility, not measured completion or a fixed estimate. The immediate business goal is to turn years of building into revenue by pursuing opportunities (“rabbits”).

The uploaded reading collection is a starting intellectual foundation for all twelve Mobs. Influences should be blended across the ensemble with intentional overlap, rather than assigning one author exclusively to one leader. Over years, the Mobs should accumulate important knowledge, learn from one another, and extract and build collective knowledge and wisdom.

“Extract” here means eliciting and organizing knowledge from people, sources, and experience for an agreed purpose. Preserve the person's meaning, ownership, confidentiality, and agency.

## Proposed 30% / 70% delivery contract
Founder opportunity packet:
- Named buyer, problem in the buyer's words, intended outcome, and next commercial action.
- Concept or prototype, what it demonstrates, and assumptions still untested.
- Agreed scope, constraints, and acceptance criteria; distinguish estimates from commitments.

Engineering acceptance:
- Confirm feasibility, dependencies, estimate, owner, and delivery scope.
- Complete backend, integrations, permissions, error handling, and deployment as required by that engagement.
- Demonstrate the agreed outcome with evidence and hand over operating instructions.

Track opportunities broadly; limit simultaneous builds to actual delivery capacity. A prototype is the start of a handoff, not evidence that production engineering is 30% complete. Proposed measures: qualified opportunities, paid starts, handoff acceptance, delivery time, accepted outcomes, margin, and reusable lessons.

## Proposed shared-learning loop
1. Capture: preserve source, date, context, permissions, and the question being answered.
2. Extract: record a short insight, supporting evidence, uncertainty, and intended use.
3. Review: have another relevant Mob challenge the insight, including counterexamples.
4. Apply: test it in a bounded task and record the result.
5. Promote: move a supported lesson into a versioned shared playbook with an owner.
6. Revisit: correct, supersede, or retire lessons as new evidence arrives.

Knowledge is what we can support and retrieve. Wisdom adds context, consequences, tradeoffs, and judgment about when a lesson applies. Repeated agreement between agents is not independent evidence.

Suggested lesson fields: ID, question, source references, source type, claim, interpretation, confidence, limits, contributing Mob, reviewer, task outcome, sharing permission, version, and superseded-by reference.

Suggested repository structure:
- memory/: concise founder decisions and context.
- characters/: draft leader profiles and relationships.
- sources/: source inventory and original commentary; full texts only when redistribution is authorized.
- knowledge/inbox/: unreviewed extractions.
- knowledge/reviewed/: supported insights and counterexamples.
- playbooks/: tested methods with applicability limits.
- handoffs/: founder-to-engineering packets.
- outcomes/: accepted results and retrospective lessons without private client material.

Shared learning is a design goal. This documentation does not deploy agents, train model weights, connect Yawdie, or create an automatic knowledge-sharing runtime.
